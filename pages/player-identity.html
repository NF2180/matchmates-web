The Durable Idea

Why Every Player Needs a Persistent Sports Identity

Scoring apps record matches. Spreadsheets track points. WhatsApp groups share results. None of them build something that belongs to the player — a cumulative record of who they are as a sportsperson, that travels with them through life.

The Problem with Ephemeral Sports Records

Most cricket scoring apps record a match and move on. The scorecard lives for a season, then disappears into a folder nobody opens. There are no personal milestones, no career averages, no record that you took 5 wickets in the 2022 society final. For weekend cricket players — in apartment complexes, on the Maidan, in local tournaments — unlimited personal history has never existed. Until now.

Imagine a player who has lived in Bangalore for 8 years, played in three different apartment complexes, and participated in hundreds of cricket and badminton matches across that time. They're a reliable mid-order bat, a sharp slipper, and a competitive badminton singles player.

Ask them for their career record. They'll shrug. There isn't one. Every move reset the clock. Every new community required re-establishing credibility from zero. Their sporting reputation — built match by match, season by season — lives nowhere except in the memories of the people who happened to be there.

This is the default state of community sports everywhere. And it doesn't have to be.

A persistent sports identity is a player's permanent, cumulative record of sporting activity — matches, statistics, rankings, achievements, and community memberships — that belongs to the player and travels with them regardless of where they live or which community they join.

What LinkedIn Did for Professional Identity

Before LinkedIn, professional credentials existed on paper CVs — documents that had to be recreated, that lived in filing cabinets, and that didn't update themselves. Every career transition required manually rebuilding trust from scratch.

LinkedIn created a persistent professional identity. Your work history, endorsements, connections, and achievements accumulate in one place. Your reputation is portable. You don't lose your professional credibility when you change companies.

Strava did the same thing for fitness activity. Every run, every ride, every training session adds to a cumulative record. The platform transforms individual activities into a narrative of athletic improvement over time. Your fitness identity is persistent — it doesn't reset when you move neighborhoods or join a new running club.

CricHeroes came close for cricket. Players who participate in league cricket have a career record: runs, wickets, averages, tournaments played. But this record is tied to formal league participation. It doesn't capture the Tuesday evening apartment cricket match. It doesn't follow you to a new city's informal cricket scene.

MatchMates is building the equivalent for all community sport — formal and informal, across all sports, across all communities, across all geographies.

What a Persistent Sports Identity Contains

Career Statistics

Every match played, every run scored, every wicket taken, every game won. Statistical records that accumulate automatically from live scoring — no manual entry, no approximation. A cricket player can see their career average across 5 years of apartment cricket. A badminton player can track their win rate progression over 200 games.

Skill Ratings

ELO-style ratings that adjust after every match based on the quality of opposition and the margin of result. Ratings that reflect current form, not just historical achievement. A player who improves rapidly sees their rating rise in real time. A player who hasn't played in 6 months sees their active rating reflect that accurately.

Community History

A record of every community the player has been part of — which societies, which clubs, which campuses, which teams. The player's community memberships become part of their identity. Organizers in a new community can see immediately what calibre of player they're inviting.

Achievements and Milestones

First century. 100 matches played. 50-match win streak in badminton. These milestones exist in the platform as permanent markers. They don't disappear when a WhatsApp group is archived or a spreadsheet is deleted.

Reputation Signals

How reliable is this player for showing up to confirmed matches? How do they perform under pressure in knockout stages? Have they organized matches for their community? A persistent identity accumulates these signals over time — creating a richer picture than any single tournament result.

Why This Is the Real Moat

Scheduling features can be copied. Scoring interfaces can be replicated. Rankings algorithms are public knowledge. But the network of player identities — each carrying years of match history, career statistics, and community connections — cannot be replicated from scratch.

The more players use MatchMates and build their identities on the platform, the more valuable the platform becomes for every player. An organizer seeking to form a balanced team has a richer talent pool to draw from. A new resident joining a community can be matched with players at their skill level immediately. A player moving to a new city can connect with the MatchMates community in their new neighborhood and be recognized for who they are — not start from zero.

This is a network effect built on identity, not just activity. It is significantly harder to displace than a feature advantage.

The Experience From a Player's Perspective

Today, your sporting life looks like this: you play, people who were there remember, and then it's gone. The match ends and the record disappears.

With persistent sports identity, your sporting life looks like this:

  • You move into a new apartment. Your profile shows your career stats, your current ELO rating, and your community history.
  • The sports organizer discovers you in the player directory, sees you're a 1,840-rated badminton player with a 61% win rate over 200 matches, and invites you to the Saturday game.
  • You play. The match is scored live. Your rating updates. Your career match count ticks over to 201.
  • Three years later, you move again. Your profile travels with you. You join the new community's sports group. No rebuilding required.

The goal is for a player's sporting reputation to be as durable and portable as their professional reputation. MatchMates is the infrastructure layer that makes this possible.

Identity Across Sports

A player who plays cricket and badminton has one identity on MatchMates — not two separate profiles on two separate apps. Their cricket record and their badminton record coexist under the same profile. Their ELO rating is sport-specific, so their cricket ranking doesn't affect their badminton standing. But their reputation as a reliable, active, and engaged community sports player is unified.

This matters because community sports is rarely mono-sport. The same residents who play cricket in winter play badminton in summer. A platform that understands this plays a fundamentally different role than one that forces a choice.

Why Games Stop Happening

The Organiser Carries Too Much

Every community has that one person who organises the cricket. They chase confirmations, form teams on instinct, keep score manually, and update a spreadsheet nobody else looks at. When they stop, the games stop. MatchMates fixes this — for the organiser and for every player who shows up.

😓

Scoring Is Manual Friction

Someone has to keep a paper book, type scores into WhatsApp, and remember who got what wicket. One distraction and the record is wrong. MatchMates scores live — tap by tap, ball by ball, automatically.

⏱️

Team Formation Takes Forever

The organiser knows maybe 12 of the 22 players well enough to pick teams fairly. The rest is guesswork. MatchMates reads player ratings and suggests balanced teams in seconds.

🗓️

Scheduling Is a Weekend Job

Confirming 22 players, booking a ground, and communicating the plan takes more effort than the match itself. MatchMates handles availability, reminders, and ground booking in one place.

🏆

Good Performances Go Unnoticed

Rahul takes 4 wickets. Everyone cheers. By Monday it's forgotten. No record, no milestone, no rank change. MatchMates makes every performance count — permanently.

📉

Players Lose Interest Over Time

Without rankings, milestones, or history, there's no reason to show up consistently. Games feel disposable. MatchMates gives players a reason to perform — because it tracks everything and compares everyone.

🔄

History Resets When You Move

Three years of weekend cricket. Hundreds of matches. Then you change apartments and your record disappears. MatchMates gives players a permanent profile that travels with them — to any community, anywhere.

How It Works

Design Principles

How We Think About
Community Sports

Every player has one identity

A player's history, rankings, achievements, and reputation belong to them — not to a society's WhatsApp group or a season's spreadsheet.

Every match becomes permanent history

Ball-by-ball records, scorecards, and team compositions are preserved forever. What happened on the pitch stays on the record.

Rankings evolve automatically

ELO ratings and ladder standings update after every match. No monthly manual effort. No stale leaderboards.

Communities should not depend on one volunteer

When the organizer moves cities, the community doesn't collapse. Institutional memory lives in the platform, not in someone's phone.

Sports data should survive relocation

Moving to a new apartment or city shouldn't reset your sporting reputation. Identity travels with the player.

Participation should increase over time

Rankings, achievements, and visible history create intrinsic motivation to play more. A good sports OS makes the community more active, not just more organized.
